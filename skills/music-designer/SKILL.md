# Music Designer

Turn the user's brief into an actionable music workflow. Identify song or instrumental use, desired length, genre, language, listening priorities, available model, budget and delivery destination. Reuse supplied decisions and choose reversible details within the authorized scope.

For Suno refinement, creator-experience research, or transferring those techniques to a local generator, read [Suno community workflow](references/suno-community-workflow.md). It contains source assessments, provider differences and comparison cases.

For section energy, score/stem diagnostics and arrangement revisions, use the bundled [song-arrangement](../song-arrangement/SKILL.md). Its tools analyze supplied scores/stems; they do not install or generate music models.

## Musical direction

- Name the strengths the listener wants to retain, such as accompaniment, hook, groove or vocal character. Use them as comparison criteria.
- Describe a coherent musical identity through groove, language, core instruments, vocal traits and production texture. Give instruments roles and specify one recognizable motif where useful.
- Separate global sound from section-specific changes. Use the target model's supported section syntax and test detailed performance cues with short comparisons.
- Check lyric phrasing against the pulse, including breath space, stress, repeated sections and meaningful line breaks. Preserve intended meaning, names and approved text. Keep canonical lyrics and experimental generation spellings traceable.
- Use descriptive musical traits and authorized references. For open-model prompts, exclude celebrity imitation and trademarked style tags. Respect voice consent and the project's persona decisions.

## Capability and provider fit

Verify the selected model version, mode, actual controls, runtime and account entitlement. Model lists and community recipes age quickly. Carry existing local-only or zero-key requirements into the plan. Paid generation, uploads and publishing follow the user's explicit authorization.

Match the edit to the available interface. Suno section replacement, voice features and sliders need current capability checks. Local generators have their own controls. YuE2 score edits produce a new complete recording, so reassess vocals and accompaniment across the output. Saved plans and latents provide narrower reuse paths when the installed runtime supports them.

Choose single generation, extension, local editing, stem work or assembly based on the model and requested duration. Inspect long-form consistency and musical joins. Set any duration or retry limit from the task and observed evidence.

## Iteration and listening

1. Keep an original baseline. Separate broad musical exploration from refinement of a promising take.
2. During refinement, change one meaningful variable and compare a bounded set of candidates within the available budget. Retain failed and completed attempts with parent IDs, inputs and settings.
3. Match listening loudness in comparison copies. Evaluate accompaniment groove, hook, lyric intelligibility, vocal suitability, instrument definition, transitions and ending. Use the user's priorities to weight the decision.
4. Use ASR or text-audio similarity to locate questions. Record human listening judgments separately from machine scores and preserve disagreement.
5. Inspect the full track, including the later chorus and tail. Promote a version only under the active project authority and record the reason for the choice.

Treat community settings as hypotheses until tested on the current model and task. Distinguish official capabilities, creator reports and local measurements. State the missing evidence when a claimed improvement has only textual support.

## Editing and delivery

Preserve strong sections and use the smallest supported edit for the identified defect. Check phrase boundaries, beat alignment, timbre and reverb tails after the edit.

Use stem separation when it serves a specific edit. Compare an aligned, original-gain recombination with the original mix before further processing. Listen for leakage, watery artifacts, transient damage and phase problems.

Choose EQ, de-essing, compression or limiting from a diagnosed need. Keep bypass comparisons and preserve the source. Record sample rate, channels, sample count, loudness, true peak and full-scale samples. Use the project media-validation rules for PCM handling. Keep float or native lossless sources available through finishing and validate the final encode.

## Rights and handoff

Check code, model-weight, input and output-use licenses separately. Record the applicable version and evidence. MusicGen's MIT code license and CC-BY-NC model weights require separate consideration. Check current provider terms and song provenance for commercial use. Keep unclear rights unresolved and follow the project's exception process.

Use authorized source recordings and lyrics. Refuse commercial uses excluded by the applicable license. Include AI-generation disclosure for vocal-music deliveries. Apply required AI disclosure and supported provenance metadata for the intended platform. Confirm any legal or watermark requirement against the actual use and jurisdiction.

Return the chosen musical direction, provider-specific inputs, bounded comparison or edit plan, success criteria, rights evidence and the next concrete artifact. When execution is requested and authorized, carry it through available checks and identify any remaining listening or access requirement.
